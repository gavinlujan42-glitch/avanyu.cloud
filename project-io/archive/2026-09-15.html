ORANGE · PERMIT-BOUNDARY WATCH

Oracle says current data-center construction is on a separate adjacent site from YGI’s proposed microgrid and is outside the stayed air-permit proceeding. That is a material party position; its verification depends on parcel, permit and activity records.

Attributable public record

Oracle’s September 14 statement says separate companies operate the campus and microgrid; county permits cover data-center buildings; and the Supreme Court stay applies to the microgrid air proceeding. Oracle also says YGI discussed preconstruction activities with NMED. NMED’s dashboard, updated September 14 at 9:00 PM, still listed permit 10883 under review.

Oracle separately disclosed that YGI retained environmental-health scientist Christopher Ollson. His comments support the fuel-cell design and report roughly 92 percent less nitrogen oxide than the prior turbine-and-diesel design, an approximately 11-million-gallon one-time non-potable fill and about 168,000 gallons per year for maintenance. These are applicant-sponsored conclusions, not agency findings.

Limits and disputed framing

Oracle’s statement that YGI is in compliance has not been adopted in a published NMED or court finding located here. Site adjacency and separate entities do not answer what physical work is occurring on each parcel. The expert’s emissions comparison is against the withdrawn design, and the cited water quantities exclude construction-phase pumping. No evidence reviewed establishes misconduct or corruption.

Infrastructure judgment

The control question is activity-by-parcel: owner and operator, permit number, contractor scope, installed equipment and emissions potential. Publish the parcel map, county permits, work logs, NMED correspondence and inspection records. Also distinguish the YGI-retained expert from the independent environmental assessor promised for future operations.

Control requests

  1. Parcel map, ownership/control records and legal descriptions.
  2. County permits, scopes of work and inspections for each site.
  3. Written NMED guidance or concurrence on preconstruction.
  4. Full expert submission with absolute emissions, methodology and engagement disclosure.
  5. Separate ledgers for construction water, one-time fills and annual operations.
