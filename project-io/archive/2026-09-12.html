A second power path enters the record.

Published September 12, 2026 · 8:09 AM MDT

ORANGE · CONTINGENCY WATCH

Oracle says Project Jupiter remains committed to its private Bloom Energy microgrid, but acknowledged that a grid provider could become the fallback if the air permit is denied. The contingency connects air permitting directly to county agreements, utility planning, rate protection and schedule risk.

Verified public record

Meeting-based reporting from September 8 quotes Oracle describing a grid-provider alternative if the on-site air permit is not approved. Doña Ana County Chair Manuel Sanchez said the IRB and community-benefit agreements were structured around on-site generation and avoiding demand on El Paso Electric; a change could require commission action.

The current Oracle-and-STACK-funded project site still promises independent on-site power, no strain on the electric grid and no effect on customer rates or grid stability. Oracle’s April announcement says up to 2.45 GW of Bloom fuel cells will fully power the campus. No utility selection, interconnection request, load study or rate filing was located.

Oracle representatives also described the previously announced $50 million state-linked water-infrastructure commitment as fully funded and expected to reach the county. A new public disbursement and receipt ledger was not located.

Claims not yet demonstrated

Oracle says a future grid arrangement should benefit existing customers rather than raise rates. Opponents warn that a connection could shift costs or reliability risks. No disclosed utility study supports either outcome yet.

This contingency is not a plan change, executed agreement or corruption evidence. No new Supreme Court merits ruling, water-meter ledger, pipeline approval or IRB ruling was found.

Infrastructure judgment

The air permit now selects between two very different delivery architectures: the natural-gas-fed private microgrid or an undefined utility-grid alternative. The second path needs its own transparent feasibility, cost-allocation, transmission, emissions and agreement analysis before schedule pressure turns it into an emergency decision.

Control requests

  1. Publish any grid-provider contacts, interconnection applications and load studies.
  2. Identify the IRB and community-benefit clauses a power change would trigger.
  3. Model who pays for generation, transmission and reliability upgrades.
  4. Publish state transfer and county receipt records for the $50 million water commitment.
  5. Keep construction water, initial fills and operational use in separate ledgers.
