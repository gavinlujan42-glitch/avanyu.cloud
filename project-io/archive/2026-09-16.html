Disclosure is a control, not a conclusion.

Published September 16, 2026 · 8:22 AM MDT

ORANGE · DISCLOSURE-PROVENANCE WATCH

A September 15 report identifies a $12,400 Oracle America contribution in Deb for New Mexico’s latest campaign filing. PROJECT IO treats it as an attributed filing claim pending primary CFIS extraction—not evidence of improper influence.

Attributable public record

The Candle identifies an August 26, 2026 contribution from Oracle America, Inc. and reproduces the reported filing entry. The New Mexico Secretary of State identifies CFIS as the official primary-record system. Independent transaction extraction was not completed during this review, so the amount remains attributed to the report.

The article’s broader claim that Oracle and BorderPlex contributions exceed $198,000 lacks a published recipient-level ledger and is not adopted as verified. NMED continues to label the YGI air proceeding as stayed.

What is not established

A campaign contribution does not establish a quid pro quo, regulatory influence, illegality or corruption. Haaland is a gubernatorial candidate, not an official presently adjudicating Jupiter’s permits. No documented connection between the reported contribution and an official Jupiter action was located.

Governance judgment

Normalize every transaction by legal contributor, affiliate, recipient, date, amount, filing, amendment and governmental role. Keep that ledger separate from permits, contracts, lobbying and IRBs until documentary evidence connects them.

Control requests

  1. Preserve the complete underlying campaign filing.
  2. Extract contributor aliases and recipient-level transactions.
  3. Identify amendments, refunds and in-kind contributions.
  4. Map only documented official acts—never assumed influence.
  5. Continue separate water, air, pipeline and IRB evidence ledgers.
